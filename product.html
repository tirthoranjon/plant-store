Product Information

Product information for plants generally falls into two categories: botanical care data (how to grow the plant) and crop/market products (goods derived from plants). Understanding plant specifications ensures successful cultivation and proper utilization

Product Information
  • Plant size options: Plant sizes are categorized by both their height and the volume/diameter of their nursery grow pots. Options typically range from small plugs and 4-inch containers for cuttings to 25+ gallon pots for mature trees.
  • Light requirements: Plant light requirements dictate a plant's ability to photosynthesize and grow. They are categorized by intensity and duration, ranging from direct sun for sun-lovers (like succulents) to low, indirect light for shade-tolerant species (like snake plants)
  • Watering requirements: Optimal watering requires supplying water deeply and infrequently, rather than splashing the surface daily. Most plants, lawns, and landscapes need about 2.5 cm (1 inch) of water weekly. Water thoroughly until the soil is moist down to 12 to 15 cm (5 to 6 inches) so it reaches deep root zones.
  • Toxicity level (especially for pets): The plant in the image is a Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum), which is entirely non-toxic to dogs and cats. According to official animal health databases like the ASPCA Pet Plant Guide, it contains no toxic chemical compounds that pose a systemic threat to domestic animals.
  • Repotting recommendations: Repot your plants only when they are root-bound—typically every 1 to 3 years. Choose a new pot just 1–2 inches larger in diameter than the current one. For best results, repot during the active growing season (spring or summer) to minimize transplant shock and encourage healthy root growth.

Plant Parent Tips Section

To be a successful plant parent, focus on getting the fundamentals right: water only when the topsoil is dry, provide the correct lighting for your specific variety, and use well-draining soil. Avoid overwatering, as it is the most common mistake and often leads to fatal root rot.
